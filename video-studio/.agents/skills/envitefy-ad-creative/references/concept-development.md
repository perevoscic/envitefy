# Concept development and critique

Use when a new campaign leaves direction open or the user requests a substantial rethink. Scale exploration to the request; a supplied concept does not need a competing pitch deck.

## Find the advertising angle

Describe who is doing what, what gets in the way, and what outcome matters. Choose one promise the demonstrated Envitefy flow supports. Separate the emotional payoff from the software action that makes it credible.

"Organize an event" is too broad to stage. "A host keeps searching old messages for the updated venue" provides an observable action, frustration, and resolution. Showing the current venue and directions supports that story; it cannot prove that every guest will arrive on time.

Keep an evidence record for the promise: catalog feature ID and availability, event type, source path or inspected UI state, and verification still needed. Do not invent savings, prices, free offers, testimonials, adoption figures, scarcity, or competitor shortcomings. Narrow unsupported wording to an observable benefit or remove it. Distinguish explicit save from publish, guest RSVP from host tracking, and calendar download from automatic synchronization.

Use current primary sources when an idea depends on platform specifications, policies, trends, competitor facts, or pricing; record URL and date checked. Identify transferable craft choices in references rather than copying another brand's script or distinctive campaign. Do not add research merely to decorate a brief with inspiration links.

## Explore distinct approaches

Choose approaches that fit the audience and request; these are possibilities, not a fixed formula:

- A recognizable human situation with a turn that makes the product useful.
- A direct demonstration where the result earns attention before explanation.
- An objection answered through visible product behavior.
- A visual metaphor or heightened scenario anchored by real product proof.
- A candid creator-style explanation, without pretending a fictional actor is a real customer giving a testimonial.
- A desirable outcome that creates interest without inventing a crisis.

For each serious candidate, record a working title, audience insight/hypothesis, opening image and line, story turn, promise, proof action/result, CTA, visual treatment, and production risk. A few distinct candidates are more useful than a quota of near-identical hooks.

Compare the premise as well as the visuals against campaign history. Different people in another group-chat panic may repeat the same idea. Preserve an explicitly requested recurring style; otherwise explain how the selected premise differs from recent work.

## Decide and repair

Use written judgments such as strong, revise, or reject, with a reason. These are creative judgments, not measured ad performance.

| Question | What should change the decision |
| --- | --- |
| Why would this audience care? | A situation or desire specific enough to stage; no demographic stereotypes standing in for insight. |
| What does the opening communicate? | A legible subject and reason to keep watching, including when muted. Surprise leads into the promise. |
| Could any app use this script unchanged? | The story needs the Envitefy action/result rather than generic "planning made easy" narration. |
| Is the promise believable? | Verified capability in the chosen flow, without jumping to an unsupported outcome. |
| Is there a story turn? | A meaningful reveal, response, or payoff. A straightforward walkthrough is appropriate when requested. |
| Can we make it convincingly? | Achievable performance and motion; exact screens/text use real captures or editable composition. |
| Does the ending follow? | One main next action and enough time/space for the studio's required brand ending. |

Repair weak ideas before requesting expensive assets:

- **Generic narration:** Replace "Planning is stressful. Envitefy makes it effortless" with a visible situation and supported action; avoid promising effortlessness.
- **Overloaded demo:** If narration names four actions while one screen flashes by, select the proof needed and give each shown action its corresponding explanation and readable state.
- **Fragile generated action:** If an actor must tap exact labels while moving a phone, plan stable framing and a real UI insert; let the actor establish the reaction.
- **Unrelated comedy:** If a joke could end with any sponsor's logo, make the product action cause or resolve the turn.

Record the chosen concept, rejected alternatives with reasons, weaknesses, actual revisions, and remaining proof/capture questions. Stop when the idea meets the brief and material weaknesses are resolved; more candidate generation needs a reason.
